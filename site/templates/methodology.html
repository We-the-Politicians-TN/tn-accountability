Every stored record keeps the original text exactly as published alongside any cleaned version, and records which downloaded file and line it came from. Where a source value cannot be read — a filing dated 29 February in a non-leap year, for instance — the original is kept and the cleaned field is left empty. We do not guess.

The in-session contribution ban, and why timing is not our signal

Tennessee bars contributions while the General Assembly is in session. The effect is not subtle. Contributions to legislators during 2025:

January$1,385,084
February$1,700
March$2,498
April$18,804
October$1,186,566
December$1,280,101

Because fundraising is pushed into the months between sessions, and bills are introduced early in session, almost every bill has contributions in the weeks before it. We tested this: one legislator's bills each showed between $40,150 and $45,250 in the preceding {{ lookback }} days — but those were the same contributions counted against every bill, because the windows overlap.

So a raw "money before the bill" figure measures the legislative calendar, not the bill. We publish it only beside the subject-matched figure, so you can see how little it separates one bill from another.

The measure we do use

For each bill a legislator primarily sponsored, we count contributions received in the {{ lookback }} days before introduction, but only from donors whose industry matches an industry the bill concerns.

  • Bill industries come from LegiScan's subject labels, mapped to a published list of industries.
  • Donor industries come from the donor's name and, in future, from Ethics Commission lobbyist-employer registrations.
  • Ceremonial resolutions are excluded. Memorials honouring constituents, sports teams and retirements carry no policy content — 36% of all bills, and over 90% of resolutions. Pairing one with a donor's industry would be meaningless.

Other ways money reaches a legislator, and what Tennessee discloses

Contributions are not the only channel. Tennessee bars lobbyists and their employers from giving legislators gifts (TCA § 3-6-305), so the individual free lunch is mostly prohibited rather than merely undisclosed. What the law permits is narrow, and it is reported: events where the entire General Assembly is invited, capped at $78 a person a day; employer-paid meals capped at $158 a person a year; and sponsored travel, which the member must declare.

Each member files a Statement of Disclosure of Interests every April under penalty of perjury, listing outside income, board positions, investments over $10,000, travel paid by others, lobbying by family members, retainers from entities that lobby, loans, and any leadership PAC they control. We show it on the member's page exactly as filed. Tennessee requires names, not amounts, so no figures appear for income or investments.

Two limits are structural. Sponsored events name the host and the cost but never who attended, because the legal condition is that everyone was invited — so they cannot be attributed to a member and we do not try. And nothing in any of these records says why a member voted; the site shows what was received or held, never motive.

Comparison to the chamber, never in isolation

Each member is compared to the median for their own chamber, because the chambers differ substantially:

Raising more than the median is common and unremarkable on its own: leadership positions and competitive districts attract money. Republicans hold both majorities and therefore most leadership roles, and their members' figures are higher on average as a result. Party is never an input to any threshold, ranking or flag — it is shown as a fact about each member, nothing more.

If you think something warrants examination

We do not make determinations about anyone's conduct. If a record here prompts a question, the Registry of Election Finance and the Tennessee Ethics Commission both accept complaints from members of the public. The relevant provisions are in Tennessee Code Annotated Titles 2, 3 and 8, and in the House and Senate ethics codes.

Corrections

If a figure here is wrong we want to know and will fix it. Open an issue — including on behalf of a legislator or their staff.

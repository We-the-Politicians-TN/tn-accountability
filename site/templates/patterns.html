Patterns for review

Read this before reading the list

Nothing on this page is an accusation. Every entry describes a pattern in public records and nothing more. There are ordinary explanations for every one of them: industries donate to the members who work on their issues, and members seek support from those who care about what they work on. That is lawful and open.

This list exists so patterns can be examined, not so conclusions can be drawn from it.

How this list is built

A legislator appears here when both of the following hold:

  1. They received contributions from donors in the same industry as a bill they primarily sponsored, within {{ lookback }} days before introduction; and
  2. Their total raised is at or above the median for their chamber.

Thresholds are stated here deliberately, and they are the same for everyone. Party is never an input. Full method.

We deliberately do not rank by contribution timing alone. Tennessee's in-session contribution ban means nearly every member raises money in the same months, so almost every bill has money behind it beforehand. Ranking on that would surface whoever introduced bills closest to the fundraising season — a quirk of the calendar, presented as though it meant something.
